How to report crypto in the Norwegian tax return

Where the figures go, how wealth is valued, and what about fees, costs and cash on exchanges.

Crypto goes in the tax return (skattemeldingen) under Finans → Virtuelle eiendeler / kryptovaluta. You can put your coins on one card, but then you must be able to show what the total consists of, coin by coin. The PDF report and the CSV files from Kryptokompis give you that specification.

The fields

Kryptokompis shows the figures under Tax report → For the tax return and in chapter 1 of the PDF report:

Field in the tax return What it is
Formuesverdi (wealth) The value of your coins at the turn of the year
Skattepliktig gevinst (taxable gain) All sales and swaps with a gain
Fradragsberettiget tap (deductible loss) All sales and swaps with a loss
Andre inntekter (other income) Staking, rewards and other income in coins
Forvaltningskostnader (management costs) Costs you entered yourself

The wealth value

Crypto is valued at the turn of the year. For the most common coins the tax return has the tax administration's own rates as of 1 January. Kryptokompis shows the value at the exchanges' rates at the turn of the year. If they differ, check which rate to use for that coin.

Gains and losses separately

Gains and losses go in separate fields. Do not net them against each other before entering them.

A swap is a sale too

Swapping one coin for another - including for a stablecoin such as USDT or USDC - is a realisation. The gain or loss is calculated in NOK on the day of the swap.

Transfers to yourself

A transfer between your own accounts and wallets is not a sale. But a fee paid in crypto is a small realisation. Kryptokompis includes it by itself.

Income and costs

Staking, rewards and mining are income at their market value on the day you received them. Costs tied to that income, such as electricity for mining, can normally be deducted.

Management costs are only deductible for what actually concerns your crypto: software and price data (for example TradingView), tools to work out the tax, and an accountant. Equipment and subscriptions you mainly use privately, such as a PC, broadband and a phone, are normally not deductible for a private person - only when the crypto trading is run as a business (sole proprietorship or AS). In Kryptokompis you enter the full cost and how large a share is for crypto.

Cash on exchanges

Dollars and kroner held on an exchange at the turn of the year are not a virtual asset, but they are wealth - normally a claim against the exchange. Where to report the amount depends on the balance, the currency and where the exchange is based. Kryptokompis shows the balance separately so you have the figure when you report it.

Keep the documentation

The PDF and CSV files are not submitted unless you are asked for them, but they are your documentation if the tax administration asks.

This is simplified guidance, not tax advice. Read more at Skatteetaten, and ask an accountant if unsure.
